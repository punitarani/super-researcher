THE APP, IN ORDER

Six stages, start to finish

SuperResearcher runs on your own computer. You describe a topic, and it does the legwork: finding real sources, reading them, organizing what they say, and writing it up as a polished report with charts pulled from the sources themselves. One page of this guide per stage, in the order you'll click them:

1

Scope

Describe the research and set the dials. The run executes here.

2

3

4

Assemble the evidence each section will cite.

5

Draft the outline, then write the paper section by section.

6

Publish

Review figures, pick a template, export PDF & HTML.

How to read this guide: each of the next six pages covers one stage — what the screen shows, what each control means and why it exists, and the one action that moves you forward.
STAGE 1 Scope tab

Scope: the research brief

Every control on the form, what it means, and why it's there. The topic is the only required field — the rest tune how the run behaves.

Research topic Required

The subject in plain language. Drives queries, source selection, topics, chapters.

Why: the app's only definition of "relevant." Specific beats clever.

Depth

Search results considered per query.

Low 3Med 7High 10X-High 15Ludicrous 50

Why: the thoroughness dial. Richer corpus, longer run.

Breadth

Angles of attack generated per topic when expanding queries.

Low 1Med 3High 5

Why: depth digs one query; breadth covers angles you wouldn't think of.

Final sources

Target corpus size. Discovery stops here (default 120, max 500).

Why: caps the run — every source is downloaded, chunked, embedded.

Research context

Why it matters, who it's for, what decision it supports.

Why: an investor's report reads differently from a founder's. Context picks which.

Scope controls (advanced)

Audience, geography, time horizon, objective, must include / exclude.

Why: guardrails — "must exclude: consumer drones" keeps the report on rails.

STAGE 2 Map tab

Map: the embedding Atlas

Every passage becomes an embedding — a numeric fingerprint of what it means — plotted on a 2D map. Dots together say similar things. Why embeddings, not keywords: keywords find only words you thought of; embeddings find meaning — "battery energy density" lands beside "cell specific energy" with zero shared words. Topic discovery sees the real themes, and every citation is retrieved by meaning.

STAGE 3 Curate tab

Curate: shape the topic tree

The Atlas clusters become a topic tree — the chapters and subtopics your report could cover. This is your editorial pass: Keep what matters, Reject what doesn't. Nothing rejected here reaches the report. The tree is discovered from your corpus, not invented — every branch traces back to real clusters of passages.

STAGE 4 Compose tab

Compose: brief the writer

For each section, you prepare query terms — the phrases the app uses to pull the right evidence from the Atlas at writing time. Why this stage exists: writing straight from 3,000 chunks would drown the model in irrelevant passages. Compose narrows each section to a focused evidence bundle — a few dozen top chunks — so every section is written from the best material available, and every claim carries a real citation. Finalize the terms here; Compile builds on exactly this.

Lock before compiling

STAGE 5 Compile tab

Compile: write the paper

Two moves. First Plan TOC — the app drafts a chapter-by-chapter outline from your curated topics. Read it like an editor; tick Rebuild TOC and run it again until the shape is right. Then Compile paper writes every section one by one, each from its evidence bundle, each factual claim carrying an inline citation. The outline is a draft, not a commitment — rebuild as many times as you like before a word is written.

STAGE 6 Publish tab

Publish: figures, then export

Two jobs. Visual review first: the app pulls charts and figures straight out of your source PDFs and matches each to the section it belongs in — Add the good ones, Reject the duds. These are the real charts from the real sources, each captioned with where it came from. Then export: pick a template, tick your formats, hit Publish.

BEFORE YOU GO

Questions, answered

The things first-time users usually ask.

How long does a full report take?

Under an hour for a focused brief; several hours for a 50-section deep dive over 100+ sources. Live progress throughout — close the browser and come back anytime.

Which API keys do I need?

A search key (Exa or Serper) and a language-model key (Gemini, or the Codex CLI) — in .env before your first real run.

Can I see a finished report first?

Yes — the repo's outputs/ folder has complete reports, including the eVTOL air-mobility deep dive (55 sections, 11 source figures).

Where do my reports live?

research_runs/<date-topic>_Corpus/ — originals, sidecars, the Atlas, finished PDFs. Nothing locked inside the app.

What if something breaks halfway?

Each stage resumes where it stopped — completed work is never redone. Final verdicts are honest: Pass, Pass with warnings, or Fail.

Do I need to babysit it?

No. Set the brief, hit Start, walk away. The two moments that want you are Curate (your editorial pass) and Publish (figure review).
